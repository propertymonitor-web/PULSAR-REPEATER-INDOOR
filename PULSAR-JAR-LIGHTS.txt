PULSAR-REPEATER-INDOOR: JAR LIGHTS
A LODESTAR PULSAR repeater in a frosted glass jar, with lights that show what it is doing.

Imagined and engineered by: Aries Thorne & Todd Steinhauer for Wolf Reactive Systems (WRS)
Moral Support: Athena the K9

STATUS: BETA. The animation logic is tested on a PC (20,000+ checks). It has NOT been run on a real
radio board or real LEDs yet. Everything in the wiring section is standard practice for this kind of LED,
not something we have measured on this build.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THE LIGHTS DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Round 7-LED jewel, bottom of the jar, pointing up:
      HEARTBEAT. A soft red "lub-dub" every 2 seconds, with a faint glow between beats. If it stops, the
      repeater has stopped (or the lights are off).
  Stick 1 (8 LEDs) "RX", DIN at the TOP:
      A blue spot runs from the TOP to the BOTTOM each time the repeater HEARS a packet. One bright LED
      with a dimmer one behind it.
  Stick 2 (8 LEDs) "TX", DIN at the TOP:
      An amber spot runs from the BOTTOM to the TOP each time the repeater SENDS a packet.

  Good to know:
   * "Sends" means any packet the radio transmits: packets it forwards for others AND its own adverts and
     replies. We do not separate those.
   * "Hears" means any valid packet the radio receives, including copies it will not forward.
   * Busy traffic shows as a short stream of spots (up to 3 at a time on a stick), never a solid bar.
   * A sweep takes about 0.6 seconds. Very fast traffic is shown, not counted.
   * See the preview animation: jar-preview.gif.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PARTS (all 5 volt, 3-wire "NeoPixel" type: WS2812B or compatible)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   * 1 x 7-LED round jewel
   * 2 x 8-LED straight sticks
   * 1 x resistor, 300 to 470 ohm (330 is common), for the data line
   * 1 x capacitor, 100 to 470 microfarad, 6.3 volt or more, across the LED power
   * Recommended: a level shifter for the data line (for example a 74AHCT125 or 74HCT245), see 4
   * Thin wire, heat-shrink, hot glue or clear mounting tape
   Use RGB parts. LEDs that have a separate white chip (RGBW) need a small code change. Ask first.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WIRING: ONE DATA WIRE FOR ALL 23 LEDs
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   The LEDs are one chain on ONE data pin, in this order:

      board DATA pin -> [resistor] -> JEWEL DIN
      JEWEL DOUT -> RX STICK DIN (top)
      RX STICK DOUT (bottom) -> TX STICK DIN
      all 5V pins together to the 5V supply   (+ capacitor across 5V and GND at the jewel)
      all GND pins together to the board GND

   Orientation (matches the code's default): both sticks are mounted with DIN at the TOP of the jar.
   That means one wire runs from the BOTTOM of the RX stick (its DOUT) up to the TOP of the TX stick (its
   DIN). Put that wire inside the jar, against the glass.

   Shorter wiring option: mount the TX stick the other way up (DIN at the BOTTOM). Then the RX stick's DOUT
   (bottom) connects straight to the TX stick's DIN (bottom). Build with  -D LODESTAR_LIGHTS_TX_FLIPPED=1
   and the animation still runs bottom to top.

   The jewel's centre LED: the code assumes the first LED in the chain is the centre of the jewel. If the
   middle LED is not the one that beats strongest, build with -D LODESTAR_LIGHTS_JEWEL_CENTER=N (N = 0 to 6).

   Colours come out wrong (red where blue should be)? Build with -D LODESTAR_LIGHTS_ORDER=NEO_RGB (or
   NEO_BRG). To change colours, see the flags in lodestar/Lights.h.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. POWER AND SIGNAL: THE THINGS THAT GO WRONG
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   * Power the LEDs from the same 5 volt USB supply that powers the board, through the board's 5V/VBUS pin
     or a split cable. NEVER from the board's 3.3 volt pin. Join the grounds.
   * Current (estimated from typical datasheet numbers: about 20 mA per colour at full, about 1 mA per LED
     when dark). The PC simulation's busiest moment in a 60 second storm of traffic is about 67 mA at the
     default brightness, and about 160 mA at the highest brightness the `lights bright` command allows.
     A normal USB charger is far above that. These are estimates. Measure with a USB meter.
   * Data signal level: the board talks at 3.3 volts, and a 5 volt LED chip officially wants about 3.5.
     Many builds work anyway, some flicker or show random colours. Two fixes: (1) a level shifter (best),
     or (2) feed the LEDs from 5 V through one 1N5819 Schottky diode (about 4.5 V at the LEDs) so 3.3 V is
     enough. Keep the data wire short.
   * The resistor goes in the data wire close to the first LED. The capacitor goes across 5V and GND near
     the first LED. Connect GND before 5V when wiring a live board.
   * Which pin? It must be a free output pin on YOUR board, not one the radio, screen, GPS or battery
     monitor uses. Do not guess.
       XIAO ESP32S3 + Wio-SX1262 kit (the test board): use D1 (GPIO2). The kit's radio uses GPIO 7, 8, 9,
       38, 39, 40, 41 and 42, the button is GPIO21, the board LED GPIO48, and the I2C pins are D4 and D5.
       D1 is not used by any of them. Avoid D2 (GPIO3), which is a boot-strapping pin. Power the LEDs from
       the board's 5V pad (USB's 5 volts when plugged in; check Seeed's pinout), never the 3V3 pad.
       Other boards: tell us the board and we will pick and check a pin.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. RADIO AND JAR NOTES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   * LED chains switch fast and can add radio noise. Keep the LED wires well away from the antenna, and
     check: with `lights off`, then `lights on`, run the repeater's stats-radio command and compare the
     noise floor. If it gets noticeably worse with the lights on, move the wires or lower the brightness.
   * Glass does not block radio. Metal does: do not use a metal lid or foil near the antenna. A plastic or
     wooden lid, or an antenna outside the jar on a short lead, is safer. Test range before and after.
   * Frosted glass spreads the light nicely: aim the jewel upward from the bottom.
   * Leave the jar open enough to let a little warmth out, and keep it dry: it is an INDOOR build.
   * Keep a USB path to the board for updates.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. BUILDING IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   On GitHub: Actions -> "Build LODESTAR PULSAR firmware" -> Run workflow. Pick the board, and put the LED
   data pin in "Jar lights: the LED data pin". Leave it empty for a build with no lights.

   By hand (needs PlatformIO):
      git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/meshcore-dev/MeshCore.git
      python tools/apply_lodestar.py --meshcore MeshCore
      python tools/persist_ble_pin.py --meshcore MeshCore
      python tools/apply_lights.py --meshcore MeshCore --env <your repeater env> --pin <data pin>
      cd MeshCore && pio run -e <your repeater env>

   Commands on the repeater's serial console or remote admin (they are not saved; at reboot it goes back
   to the build's settings):
      lights                 shows on or off and the brightness
      lights on | lights off
      lights bright 0-100    percent of the highest allowed brightness

   Build settings (add to the environment as  -D NAME=value):
      LODESTAR_LIGHTS_BRIGHT      normal brightness, 0-255, default 40
      LODESTAR_LIGHTS_BRIGHT_MAX  highest the command can set, default 120 (protects the power supply)
      LODESTAR_LIGHTS_GLOW        faint glow between beats, 0-20, default 2 (0 = dark)
      LODESTAR_LIGHTS_TX_FLIPPED, LODESTAR_LIGHTS_JEWEL_CENTER, LODESTAR_LIGHTS_ORDER
      LODESTAR_LIGHTS_HEART_RGB, LODESTAR_LIGHTS_RX_RGB, LODESTAR_LIGHTS_TX_RGB   e.g. "255, 40, 70"

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT IS NOT PROVEN
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   * No real board or LED has run this. First power-up should be on the bench, with the jar open.
   * The LED update briefly pauses the processor (a fraction of a millisecond on some boards). We expect
     that to be harmless for the radio but have not measured it. Watch for missed packets in testing.
   * Whether a 3.3 volt data line is clean enough for your particular LEDs, and the noise effect on your
     radio, depend on your parts and wiring.
